# Re-ID v2 (top-view domain): research report, 2026-09-07

**Scope.** Session-scoped person re-association for hospital waiting rooms: a person leaves frame and returns minutes to about 2 hours later, same day, clothing unchanged; a human confirms every proposal on a dashboard. Cameras are indoor, fixed, elevated 30-60 degrees down. Appearance/clothing embeddings from person crops only. The report consolidates several survey passes, each independently verified against primary sources; where verification corrected or refuted an earlier position, the corrected position is what appears below and the disagreement is stated.

**Status of numbers.** Every accuracy figure is cited to its source. No public dataset exists that matches indoor 30-60 degrees elevated RGB with cross-camera identity labels, so **all absolute numbers in this report come from eye-level or aerial benchmarks and are upper bounds until re-measured on this project's own footage.** Where a target range is given for this case it is labelled as an inference, not a citation.

---

## 1. Summary

**Status, recorded 2026-09-07.** The Phase A recipe below has since been run and measured. The record is `final_models/reid/README.md`: OSNet-x1_0 from ImageNet-only weights, checkpoint sha256 beginning `66623239`, ONNX beginning `60164a99`, general-protocol mAP 0.204782 and rank-1 0.490506 on the MEVID test split, below the external same-clothes floor of 0.39. Two of the positions taken below did not survive: the mesh-diversity audit of 2026-09-03 (`research/reid-smartspaces-mesh-audit.md`) excluded NVIDIA PhysicalAI-SmartSpaces from identity-discriminative embedder training, and the supervised stage of record trained on the full MEVID train split under a softmax recipe rather than the same-clothes protocol subset proposed here. The survey itself stands; each superseded position is marked where it appears.

- **Work can start with zero new data and zero licence risk.** Train an OSNet-x1_0 appearance embedder from its in-repo **ImageNet-only** weights (torchreid MIT, `osnet_x1_0_imagenet.pth`, https://github.com/KaiyangZhou/deep-person-reid) on the **CC BY 4.0** MEVID data, then adapt unsupervised on this project's own unlabelled footage (a stage that depends on project-owned footage, which POLICY.md, rule 4 bars, so it is not actionable unless an owner lifts that bar) with Cluster Contrast (MIT, https://github.com/alibaba/cluster-contrast-reid) plus per-camera feature debiasing (ICLR 2025 Spotlight, https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10195). That last item is about 50 lines and is the single highest-leverage change found in the whole sweep: **+19.3 mAP on MSMT17** for Cluster Contrast (29.8 to 49.1, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.10195) and **about plus 9 mAP at test time on unseen domains** with **no training at all**, needing only about 100 unlabelled crops per camera to calibrate.
- **The camera angle is cheap; resolution and occlusion are not.** The only clean controlled ablation isolating pitch from distance is VReID-XFD: A to G mAP falls **29.11% to 25.73% going 30 degrees to 90 degrees pitch (3.38 pts)** while altitude/distance collapses it **33.22% to 17.66% above 80 m** (https://arxiv.org/html/2601.01312). This deployment sits at the benign end of pitch and in the near-field, so engineering effort belongs on crop resolution, seated/chair occlusion and tracklet aggregation, **not** on view-alignment machinery: GSAlign's alignment module buys +21.76 R1 cross-view and **exactly +0.00 R1 same-view** (80.00 to 80.00, https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22268), and AG-ReID.v2's purpose-built Elevated-View Attention contributes only +1.33-1.67% mAP (https://arxiv.org/html/2401.02634v1).
- **Realistic expectation (inference, not a citation).** For same-day, unchanged-clothing, tracklet-level queries against a live session gallery of about 20-60 people: **rank-1 about 75-90%, mAP about 60-80%, rank-5 recall about 92-98%**, degrading to **rank-1 about 55-70%** under crowding, heavy seated occlusion, or near-uniform clothing (scrubs, dark coats). Anchors: MEVID's published **same-clothes** floor is CAL **39.0% mAP** on a 54-ID / 113-outfit test set with clothes-change distractors (vs 4.3% on the clothes-changing protocol, https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.04656); unsupervised PCL-CLIP reaches **88.4 mAP / 94.8 R1** on Market-1501 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17218); tracklet-based SSR-C reaches **78.7 mAP** on MARS vs 85.1 supervised (https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14261); TVRID's RGB track median was **91.92% CMC@1** but on a saturated 88-identity closed set (https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04977). **Do not quote the 99-100% TVRID or 94% NVIDIA TAO headline figures anywhere externally**; see the FTC deception exposure in section 4.
- **The biggest landmine is weights, not data.** Essentially every off-the-shelf re-ID checkpoint is trained on Market-1501, MSMT17, DukeMTMC or LUPerson. LUPerson states **"LUPerson can only be used for research, commercial usage is forbidden"** (https://github.com/DengpanFu/LUPerson) and that taint travels into SOLIDER, TransReID-SSL and PersonViT weights regardless of their Apache-2.0/MIT *code* badges; TransReID-SSL's own README records that its authors do not hold the copyright of the LUPerson dataset (https://github.com/damo-cv/TransReID-SSL). MSMT17's release agreement states **"will not be used for any commercial purpose in any form"** and additionally bars showing its images in **any** publication or demonstration (https://www.pkuvmc.com/agreement/RELEASE_AGREEMENT-MSMT17.pdf). Even NVIDIA's commercially-EULA'd TAO ReIdentificationNet lists **751 Market-1501 identities** in its supervised mix and draws its roughly 3.2M-crop self-supervised pool "from Market-1501, Open Images, etc." (https://docs.nvidia.com/tao/archive/5.3.0/text/model_zoo/cv_models/reidentificationnet_transformer.html); that tension is a question for counsel, not an engineering question.
- **The second landmine is legal-not-technical, and it is patent + FTC, not BIPA.** US 11176382 "System and method for person re-identification using overhead view images" plus a cluster of waiting-room patient-identification patents (US 10832035, US 10997397, US 11232287) sit directly on the product framing (https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11176382). Separately, the FTC's biometric definition reaches **"data that depict or describe physical, biological, or behavioral traits… of or relating to an identified or identifiable person's body"** and its Policy Statement **expressly names "embedding"** as biometric information (https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/policy-statement-federal-trade-commission-biometric-information-section-5-federal-trade-commission). The Rite Aid order defines the regulated system as one producing an Output **"notwithstanding any assistance by a human being in such analysis or use"**, so **the human-in-the-loop step is a mitigation, not an exemption** (https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/DE019-StipulatedOrderforPermanentInjunctionandOtherRelief.pdf).

## 3. Recommended plan

### Phase A, $0 to $20, no new capture

**A1. Backbone and embedder (clean provenance, CPU-viable).**
Ship **OSNet-x1_0 initialized from torchreid's in-repo ImageNet weights**: MIT code, and the in-repo `pretrained=True` path resolves to `<key>_imagenet.pth`, i.e. **no re-ID dataset provenance** (https://github.com/KaiyangZhou/deep-person-reid). 2.2 M params / 0.98 GFLOPs at 256x128; Intel's OmniScaleNet derivative proves sub-0.2-GFLOP re-ID deploys (0.183 MParams / 0.174 GFLOPs, 86.1% R1 Market-1501, https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/open_model_zoo). For a second-stage reranker over a shortlist, initialize from **DINOv2 ViT-S/14, Apache-2.0 for code *and* weights, trained on Meta's own LVD-142M** (https://github.com/facebookresearch/dinov2), the cleanest licence position in the survey.

**Do not** put CLIP-ReID, SOLIDER, TransReID-SSL, PersonViT, FastReID model-zoo, NVIDIA TAO, or any KPR checkpoint in the shipped path. Do not use **KPR** at all: Hippocratic HL3-LAW-MEDIA-MIL-SOC-SV section 3.1.13 excludes any licensee that "provides good or services to… any local, state, or federal law enforcement agency", section 5.2 pushes the licence onto derivatives, and section 8.3 voids all rights if the ethical standards are found unenforceable (https://firstdonoharm.dev/version/3/0/law-media-mil-soc-sv.txt). Do not vendor **BoxMOT** (AGPL-3.0).

**Do not expect a foundation model to work untrained.** Frozen zero-shot mAP on Market-1501: DINOv2-B14 **1.71**, DINOv2-L14 1.40, CLIP-B/16 **0.43**, SigLIP2-384 5.97 (arXiv:2601.20598, verified real, Balasubramanian, 28 Jan 2026). Identity-discriminative training does about 100% of the work. The independent control is decisive: MoCo trained on ISR's *identical* video corpus scores 10.5 R1 / 2.6 mAP on Market vs ISR's 85.1 R1 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08887). Instance discrimination and identity discrimination are not interchangeable.

**A2. Permissive supervised stage.**
As run, and superseding the plan this section originally carried: the supervised stage of record trained on the **full MEVID train split** (CC BY 4.0 video), 104 identities, 485 outfits and 6,338 tracklets, under a softmax cross-entropy recipe, not on the same-clothes protocol subset. TRAINING.md gives the commands and `final_models/reid/README.md` records the run. **NVIDIA PhysicalAI-SmartSpaces is not used for this stage**, since the mesh audit of 2026-09-03 excludes it from identity-discriminative embedder training. **MEVA's indoor fixed-camera subset** remains the candidate unlabelled in-domain data and was not used in the run of record. Budget the MEVID date-mapping work honestly (see caveat C1).

**A3. Camera debiasing, the first step to take and the cheapest large win.**
Subtract the per-camera mean (and scale) from every embedding before clustering, and discard every single-camera cluster. Cluster Contrast MSMT17 mAP **29.8 to 49.1 (+19.3)**; PPLR 27.2 to 40.7; PPLR-CAM 40.7 to 44.4. Applied **at test time on unseen domains with no training**: CC 22.5 to 30.0, TransReID-SSL 53.6 to 62.3, SOLIDER 72.4 to 79.2 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.10195). Calibration cost, as published: *"using more than 25 samples per camera leads to positive effects and the gains start to be saturated over 100 samples"*, so a new hospital install needs **about 100 unlabelled crops per camera, no labels, no training**. Use centering+scaling, not ZCA whitening. Structurally this matters here because untreated pipelines run 35-80% single-camera clusters, and **a patient who has not yet returned is exactly a single-camera cluster**.

**Caution:** reimplement from the paper (about 50 lines). Code exists only in the ICLR supplementary bundle with no licence grant, and the authors are **mAy-I Inc., a commercial vendor in this space**, so route to counsel for a freedom-to-operate check alongside the patents in section 4.

**A4. Unsupervised adaptation on project-owned footage.**
This stage depends on project-owned footage, which POLICY.md, rule 4 bars, keeping self-recorded footage out of this project with "no test-camera purchase, no mounting, no recording sessions, no labelling loop over footage recorded for this project", so it is not actionable unless an owner lifts that bar. The method record below stands as written.
Cluster Contrast (MIT) or O2CAP camera-aware proxies (MIT) for images; **SSR-C (MIT) for tracklets**, which is the route that uses the project's own tracker tracklets as weak labels, done properly, using no identity labels, no camera labels and no auxiliary info, reaching **78.7 mAP / 85.6 R1 on MARS vs 85.1 supervised (92.5%)** (https://github.com/Darylmeng/SSRC-Reid). Take its NFTP module specifically (**+10.4 mAP on MARS alone**): filter noisy/occluded frames inside a tracklet, cut it into temporally-ordered sub-tracklets rather than mean-pooling, then use the parent tracklet as a free positive signal for merging. **Directly relevant here:** on Duke-V (avg 167.6 frames/tracklet vs MARS 59.5) plain InfoNCE on whole tracklets *fails to converge*, and a patient loitering in a waiting room produces exactly those long tracklets.

Recipe details worth taking as-is: prefer **InfoMap over DBSCAN** (fewer hyperparameters, beat DBSCAN on all five datasets, +3.6 mAP MSMT17); **IBN-ResNet or a SwAV-initialized ResNet** over ImageNet-supervised ResNet-50 (+8.6 and +5.2 mAP MSMT17 respectively, and SwAV also sidesteps the ImageNet-label provenance question); **do not under-batch** (32 to 256 moves Market 75.9 to 82.1 mAP). Do **not** pull the `data.zip` bundle linked from the Cluster Contrast README; it redistributes Market-1501, MSMT17 and the withdrawn DukeMTMC.

**A5. Face-stripping at ingest (legal + free).**
Blur or zero the face region before embedding. Measured cost across 6 models by 5 datasets: mAP **58.07 to 56.68** blurred, recovering to **57.44** when trained on anonymized data (**minus 0.63 net**); rank-1 minus 0.44 net; zeroing costs only 0.25% more than blurring (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.06307). This converts the suggestion that the model might be reading the face from an assumption a plaintiff can attack into a **testable, documentable design fact**, and the FTC's Rite Aid order expects exactly this kind of evidence ("competent and reliable scientific evidence", tested "under conditions that materially replicate the conditions under which the system is actually used").

**A6. Hungarian one-to-one assignment at matching time.**
A global bipartite assignment over the score matrix instead of nearest-neighbour: TVPR2 R1 99.5 to 100.0, and in the hard cross-domain case BIWI 67.9 to 85.7 (+17.8) (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23230). It is a matching step, not a biometric, so it is clean under the standing constraints. **Caution:** its published gains are measured mostly on RGB-D and depth-only branches, so treat the magnitude as directional.

**A7. Sanity floor.** Implement LOMO-style part-based colour histograms (Market R1 43.8 / mAP 22.2 on a 750-ID gallery, https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2015/papers/Liao_Person_Re-Identification_by_2015_CVPR_paper.pdf). Zero licence exposure, sub-millisecond on CPU, and against a 20-40 person session gallery its discriminative burden is much lower than that benchmark implies. Use it as a fast pre-filter and as the floor the learned model must beat.

**Phase A compute:** OSNet fine-tune on about 150 K crops takes about 3-6 GPU-hours; Cluster Contrast 50 epochs on about 30 K crops takes about 6-10 GPU-hours; camera debiasing costs about 0 GPU-hours (statistics only); SSR-C's clustering machinery processed 8,298 MARS tracklets in **under 9 seconds on one GTX 1080 Ti**. Total well inside the capacity of a rented Blackwell-class single-GPU pod, so the $0 to $20 constraint is met on compute already budgeted.

**Phase A target (inference):** rank-1 **70-85%**, mAP **55-70%**, rank-5 recall **90-96%** on a 20-60 person same-session gallery, before any project-owned labelled data exists. **Tested and not met.** The Phase A run of record, embedder ONNX `60164a99` from checkpoint `66623239`, measured general-protocol rank-1 49.05 and mAP 20.48 on the MEVID test split, below the external same-clothes floor of 39.0 mAP (`final_models/reid/README.md`). The range above was a projection and is left standing as one, labelled by the measurement that refuted it.

### Phase B, after the own-camera shoot

Everything in this section is contingent on an owner decision that has not been taken: the own-camera shoot it depends on is barred by POLICY.md, rule 4, which keeps self-recorded footage out of this project with "no test-camera purchase, no mounting, no recording sessions", so nothing below is actionable unless that bar is lifted.

**What the shoot buys that nothing public does:** the actual mount geometry (2.7-3.5 m, 30-60 degrees pitch, the deployed lenses); seated-to-standing transitions; chair and desk occlusion; top-of-head/shoulder-dominant crops; the same person re-entering the **same** camera 10-120 minutes later under drifted light; and hospital-specific near-duplicate populations (scrubs, gowns, dark winter coats, blankets, wheelchairs) that no public corpus contains. It also gives consented provenance, which is the only clean chain of title available.

**How much footage.** Three separable label-free thresholds:
- *Per-camera calibration:* about 100 crops/camera, no training (A3).
- *In-domain unsupervised adaptation:* the splits that produce the 83-88 mAP figures are Market-1501 **12,936 crops / 751 IDs / 6 cams**, MSMT17 **32,621 / 1,041 / 15 cams**, MARS **8,298 tracklets / 625 IDs**. So the working scale is roughly **10-30 K person crops spanning 400-1,000 distinct people**. At about 100 patients/day and 50-200 crops each, that is **1-3 weeks of footage per site**, gathered continuously and re-clustered.
- *Pretraining from raw video:* CycAs reached 23.3 mAP / 50.8 R1 on Market cross-domain from just **6 hours** of self-collected footage, which is parity with a supervised model transferred from the wrong domain, but not deployment quality. At the top end ISR's scaling curve runs 320 K to 41 M crops and **does not saturate**, so accumulated footage keeps paying indefinitely.

**Gate the adaptation stage on a data threshold.** ISR reports that fine-tuning on too little target data is *worse* than not adapting: **75.9 R1 / 54.3 mAP with 10%-per-identity fine-tuning vs 85.1 / 65.1 direct** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08887). Keep the un-adapted model as an automatic fallback.

**Training recipe.** (i) Synthetic stage re-rendered at the deployment geometry (S1/S3 above); NVIDIA SmartSpaces is not an as-is option for this stage, since the 2026-09-03 mesh audit excludes it from identity-discriminative embedder training and leaves only positive-pair-only view-invariance pretraining and tracker-association structure prototyping; (ii) supervised stage on MEVID; (iii) ISR-style identity-discriminative pretraining on project-owned unlabelled video, bipartite-matching crops between frame *t* and *t+δ* with a contrastive loss and reliability weighting, **reimplemented from the paper, since the ISR repo contains no training code (only README.md, demo/, demo_A.py) and the released weights are unlicensed and YouTube-scraped**; (iv) SSR-C tracklet partitioning + Cluster Contrast + camera debiasing on project-owned footage; (v) part-based head over torso/upper-body/head-top with **explicit supervised visibility weights**; the recurring finding is that implicit attention is insufficient and occluded regions leak noise into the pooled embedding (PAB-ReID reports +11.2 R1 / +13 mAP over global pooling, https://arxiv.org/html/2404.03443v1; note that this was measured on DukeMTMC derivatives, so adopt the architecture and re-benchmark on project-owned data). Keep parts as **appearance pooling only**; anything that regresses limb geometry or body proportions enters the FTO-gated zone.

**Phase B compute:** 20-60 GPU-hours for the full stack, plus rendering time if the synthetic corpus is built (ClonedPerson measured 44.8 s of compute per character end-to-end; rendering, not code, is the long pole).

**Phase B target (inference):** rank-1 **80-92%**, mAP **70-85%**, rank-5 recall **95-99%** in normal conditions; **rank-1 55-70%** under crowding/heavy seated occlusion/uniform clothing. Because a human confirms every match, **optimize rank-5/rank-10 recall and calibrated score quality, not autonomous rank-1**, and note the result from the tracking literature that naive linear fusion of appearance + motion *degrades* performance from visual noise, while a cascaded design (re-ID only as a second stage on lost tracks) gave just **+0.27 IDF1** over geometry alone (54.63 to 54.90, arXiv:2606.07233).

### Where the sweeps disagreed: read these before trusting a number

- **C1. MEVID has no per-tracklet date field.** One sweep's headline claim was that MEVID natively encodes person/outfit/camera/**date**, enabling a same-day filter for free. Verification found `track_{train,test}_info.txt` has **five columns** (start_index, end_index, person_id, outfit_id, camera_id) and chip filenames encode no date either; date exists only as a coarse train/test split property. **Budget real engineering** to map tracklets back to timestamped MEVA source clips. Offsetting good news: MEVID *does* publish an SC/CC/general protocol, so the same-clothes filter itself is free, and **the right floor to beat is 39.0% mAP (CAL, SC), not the 27.1% general figure**.
- **C2. The claim that a steep angle costs about 2 R1 and about 7 mAP is not supportable.** Derived from CARGO Aerial to Aerial against Ground to Ground, but the protocols are not comparable (60 query IDs / 134 images vs 89 / 178; gallery 18,444 vs 32,268 images) and the Aerial to Aerial R1 reads **exactly 80.00** for three different architectures, a saturated, quantized metric. HiHR also contradicts the ordering (A to G 75.53 R1 *beats* A to A 75.00). **VReID-XFD's 3.38-pt pitch penalty is the surviving evidence.** What *does* survive from CARGO is the alignment ablation (+21.76 R1 cross-view, +0.00 same-view).
- **C3. TVRID.** One sweep flagged it only as an unverified lead, near-nadir and depth-adjacent, not to be pursued; the high-angle sweep verified it in full and confirmed that **Camera 4 is described as an elevated oblique viewpoint** and that the Zenodo record carries CC BY 4.0. The verified position stands, but the set remains a smoke test, not training data.
- **C4. PeopleSansPeople.** Ranked as the cleanest licence in the whole survey; **refuted**, because the 28 humans are RenderPeople scans Unity cannot redistribute, and the re-riggable template ships four identical placeholders.
- **C5. Synthetic-only QAConv transfer.** Reported as 94% of real-data performance; against the same table's best real-data row (MSMT-all, 56.9 mAP) it is **82%**. Budget a shortfall of about 18 points, not about 6.
- **C6. NVIDIA SmartSpaces as an exact match for this vertical.** Corrected: one hospital scene, validation split only, gone from the 2026 release; zero retail. The phrase no personal data is **not on the dataset card**; only the synthetic-generation statement is.
- **C7. "Appearance beats shape."** The 67.9% vs 25.0% comparison is **RGB-D vs depth-only**, not RGB-only vs depth. It shows depth alone generalizes badly across viewpoint; it does *not* prove appearance-only at or above RGB-D. The independent TVRID medians (RGB 91.92% vs depth 30.55% CMC@1) are the better support.
- **C8. Market-1501's licence.** Its official page carries **no licence text at all**; the research-only restriction lives in a readme.txt inside the archive. An unlicensed dataset is a *worse* procurement position than a restrictively-licensed one, not a better one.
- **C9. SAIVT-SoftBio's download and instrument.** The GitHub download URL is dead (QCIF's qcloud service is decommissioned); the working route is QUT's Research Data Finder mirror. QUT's three pages name **three subtly different licence instruments** (3.0-AU, 3.0-Unported via the README hyperlink, and bare CC-BY-SA in RDF); resolve before relying on ShareAlike scope.
- **C10. Camera mounting angle is undocumented everywhere.** Not one real dataset in this survey publishes mounting height or pitch. Every `angle_fit` rating in section 2 is inference from venue type. Since the camera specification *is* an angle specification, **a sample-frame spot-check is the cheapest de-risking step available** and should happen before any dataset commitment. It was taken for NVIDIA SmartSpaces on 2026-09-03, and that pass excluded the corpus from identity-discriminative embedder training on appearance grounds rather than on geometry (research/reid-smartspaces-mesh-audit.md), so what remains open is the geometry check on whichever corpus is committed to next.

## 4. Open questions

### Owner decisions (accept or reject)

1. **Accept the two-phase plan as scoped?** Phase A ships on ImageNet-init OSNet + CC BY 4.0 data + unsupervised adaptation, with a hard denylist on LUPerson/Market/MSMT/Duke-derived checkpoints. This costs about 10 to 20 mAP versus the public state of the art and buys a defensible chain of title.
2. **Whether MEVID's *identity annotations*, and not only the MEVA video, are CC BY 4.0.** The repository has no LICENSE file. Under the standing acquisition rule no clarification request is made to a dataset owner and none was made; the ambiguity is recorded here for counsel to resolve at ship time.
3. **Two further recorded licence ambiguities, neither of them raised with the owner:** RandPerson (does Apache-2.0 extend to the off-repo image data?) and MakeHuman (does a modified-MassProduce batch export still qualify for the CC0 export exception, or do exported meshes fall back to AGPL3?). The second is a **gating** item for the roll-your-own route.
4. **Reject MTMMC and ALERT Airport for now?** ALERT needs Industry Advisory Board membership or a DHS referral plus a 3-4 week Northeastern NDA; MTMMC suspended access for about 4 months in 2025-26 over a redistribution breach by *approved* users, and has no public consent provenance for 3,669 subjects. Both are business and legal decisions, not engineering ones. Also weigh the optics of DHS-funded airport surveillance provenance inside a privacy-preserving healthcare product.
5. **Fund the own-camera shoot at 1-3 weeks per site, and accept a face-strip-at-ingest policy** costing about 0.6 mAP? Both are the load-bearing items for the legal posture, not just the accuracy one.
6. **Accept two confirmers or one?** The strongest existing standard (EU AI Act Art. 14(5)) requires identification be "separately verified and confirmed by **at least two natural persons** with the necessary competence, training and authority." The current design assumes one. This is a staffing and user-experience decision.
7. **Accept a hard marketing-language rule?** No "100% non-biometric", no "anonymous", no borrowed benchmark numbers (94% TAO, 100% TVRID). Vendors use exactly that phrasing (Trigo: "Using 100% non-biometric data"), and under the FTC's definition **the claim itself becomes the Section 5 deception exposure** independent of the technology.
8. **Reject the shortcut comparables?** A claim of similarity to Avigilon, BriefCam or Verkada needs precision: **Verkada's People-of-Interest is face-based** (its docs instruct users to upload "a photo that clearly shows the face of only one person"), BriefCam ships face recognition alongside Appearance Similarity, Avigilon markets FR next to Appearance Search, and **Canon owns Axis, Milestone and BriefCam**, so three of the five nominally independent vendors are one company. The accurate claim is that these are face-free appearance-search *features* inside suites that also ship face recognition.

### For counsel

1. **Weights taint.** Does an Apache-2.0/MIT *code* licence cure weights trained on a corpus whose owner states "commercial usage is forbidden" (LUPerson) or "will not be used for any commercial purpose in any form" (MSMT17)? Specifically: SOLIDER, TransReID-SSL, PersonViT, CLIP-ReID, FastReID model zoo. The working assumption is **no**; confirmation is requested, together with the transitive rule that fine-tuning from a tainted checkpoint inherits the restriction.
2. **NVIDIA TAO.** NVIDIA distributes ReIdentificationNet under a commercial Model EULA while its own model card lists 751 Market-1501 identities in the supervised mix and draws its roughly 3.2M-crop self-supervised pool from Market-1501 among others. Does NVIDIA's grant insulate a downstream commercial licensee from Market-1501's restriction? The EULA text sits behind an NGC click-through and could not be read without accepting terms.
3. **A CC BY 4.0 licence is not consent.** MEVID/MEVA and TVRID are CC BY 4.0, but section 2(b)(1) states "**publicity, privacy, and/or other similar personality rights**" are not licensed, and section 5 disclaims all warranties. TVRID is identifiable footage of 88 people collected in France (GDPR special-category territory). MEVA has documented IRB oversight and signed actor consent that showed participants a CC-BY-4.0 sample licence; TVRID has no equivalent public record. Is a copyright licence sufficient for a commercial healthcare product?
4. **SAIVT-SoftBio ShareAlike.** Does training a model on a CC BY-SA 3.0 database create an "adaptation" triggering ShareAlike on a derived embedding? And which instrument applies (3.0-AU vs 3.0-Unported vs bare CC-BY-SA, on which QUT's own pages disagree)? The conservative posture is evaluation-only, and whether that removes the question is the point to confirm.
5. **MakeHuman AGPL3.** Does a batch export produced via a *modified* community plugin forfeit the CC0 export exception, pushing AGPL3 copyleft onto exported meshes in a commercial product? MakeHuman's two official pages conflict (one states CC0 flatly, the operative one conditions it on an "OFFICIAL and UNMODIFIED" build).
6. **BIPA.** (a) Does the **health care exclusion** (740 ILCS 14/10) cover a waiting-room deployment given that Mosby paragraph 57 expressly disclaims a "broad, categorical exclusion", and given that waiting rooms contain non-patients (visitors, companions, vendors, staff)? Does it travel to this project as a **vendor**, or only to the covered-entity hospital? (b) If not, does the closed-list argument hold, including the statute's own sentence that "**Biometric information does not include information derived from items or procedures excluded under the definition of biometric identifiers**", given that "photographs" and "physical descriptions" are both excluded identifiers? Zellmer is 9th Circuit (persuasive only in the 7th), is record-bound, and turned on Meta *disclaiming* identification, whereas this product's stated purpose **is** to identify the same person. Daichendt survived dismissal in 2023 after plaintiffs amended to allege CVS held their contact information, which maps precisely onto a hospital that knows every patient's identity from registration.
7. **FTC.** Does a session-scoped clothing embedding fall inside "data that depict or describe physical… traits… of or relating to an identified or identifiable person's body", given the Policy Statement expressly names "embedding"? Does the intended use fall inside "Security or Surveillance Purpose" (which includes "tracking individuals' location or behavior without Affirmative Express Consent")? Is the ABSSS carve-out for "a camera… that is used to capture images… **not collected or used in connection with the generation of an Output**" reachable by any product design? The negative needs confirming too: human confirmation is not an exemption, per "notwithstanding any assistance by a human being."
8. **HIPAA.** Is a session-scoped appearance embedding a "biometric identifier" under 45 CFR 164.514(b)(2)(i)(P), a "comparable image" under (Q), or an "other unique identifying… characteristic, or code" under (R)? Can a session ID be engineered to live inside the **paragraph (c) re-identification-code** path instead of breaking Safe Harbor under (R)? Also: BAA scope, embedding ownership at termination, whether embeddings may be used for model improvement, subprocessor coverage, and whether a leaked embedding store is a reportable breach of unsecured PHI.
9. **Multi-state.** Texas CUBI is AG-enforced with no private right of action but produced $1.4B (Meta) and $1.375B (Google) settlements, a one-large-action risk profile that changes insurance/indemnity design. Washington MHMDA has a private right of action and its "identifier template can be extracted" clause attaches specifically to **face/iris/etc. imagery**; does face-blurring at ingest defeat that hook? MHMDA excludes employees; Colorado HB 24-1130 **includes** them, so hospital staff have independent rights in CO but not WA. CCPA's chapeau requires the characteristics be "used or… intended to be used… to establish individual identity", which the stated purpose here satisfies on its face. The Clearview settlement of $51.75M and 23% equity is not a usable risk benchmark, because the Seventh Circuit vacated approval in Weissman v. Clearview AI, No. 25-1673 (July 13, 2026).
10. **Patents and freedom to operate.** An opinion is needed on **US 11176382** ("person re-identification using overhead view images") and **US 10832035 / 10997397 / 11232287** (waiting-room subject and patient identification; one of which reasons explicitly that face detection fails in waiting rooms because subjects do not look at the camera). This report covers titles and abstract-level framing only and does not assess claim scope, priority dates, assignees or status. The freedom-to-operate check extends to **mAy-I Inc.**, the source of the camera-debiasing method proposed for reimplementation, and to any Kitware or Northeastern filings. This is the highest-severity non-technical risk in the report, and the standing ban on gait and body-shape signals already indicates patent sensitivity in adjacent space.
11. **Human-in-the-loop design for legal effect.** Every precedent conditions the benefit on the reviewer being trained, competent, and genuinely able to override; the Rite Aid order mandates annual operator training on "automation bias and confirmation bias" and on limitations including "**the passage of time since the capture of a Biometric Information sample**", which is precisely the minutes-to-2-hours gap here. The design consequence to confirm is that every proposal, every human decision, the reviewer identity and the latency are logged. An unlogged human step is indistinguishable from no human step after the fact.

## 5. Standing exclusions, and the datasets they reach

The rules themselves are POLICY.md's and are not restated here. What this survey adds is the list of named datasets each rule reaches.

1. **No fisheye or omnidirectional data** (POLICY.md, rule 1). Named here: CEPDOF, HABBOF, FRIDA, WEPDTOF, MW-R. UAV-Human's fisheye video modality must be excluded at ingest if that dataset is ever revisited. None of them was fetched or recommended anywhere in this survey.
2. **No gait, pose-dynamics or body-shape identity signals** (POLICY.md, section 4, which bars pose, gait, body-shape and anthropometric signals for identity). This reaches, concretely: TVPR's 7 anthropometric features (floor-to-head distance, floor-to-shoulder distance, head surface area/circumference, shoulder circumference, shoulder breadth, thoracic anteroposterior depth); the OR re-ID method's 3D point-cloud "shape and articulated motion patterns"; VReID-XFD's winning SMPL shape prior; SMPL/SMPL-X/BEDLAM entirely (BEDLAM's licence separately bars training systems for **surveillance** use, which names this application); depth and thermal channels used as shape signals (TVRID, MMPTrack, BIWI, MTMMC); SAIVT-SoftBio's soft-biometric attribute annotations; and any part-based head that regresses limb geometry or body proportions rather than pooling appearance. **KPR is out on licence grounds regardless**, which avoids litigating whether its keypoint-prompted attention counts.
3. **No face signals of any kind** (POLICY.md, rule 2). Named here: MEVID's "actor check-in photos" package is never downloaded. Blur or zero the face region **at ingest, before embedding** (cost about 0.6 mAP). Verkada and BriefCam are face-based products and are not comparables for this product.
4. **No non-commercially-licensed or unlicensed data in the shipped path, and the same bar travels to weights** (POLICY.md, section 3 for acquisition, POLICY.md, rule 11 for appearance-embedding weight provenance). Excluded from training and from anything derived: LUPerson / LUPerson-NL, Market-1501, MSMT17, DukeMTMC and all derivatives (Occluded-Duke, P-DukeMTMC, DukeMTMC-VideoReID, Duke-V), PRW, MARS, CUHK-SYSU, CARGO, DetReIDX, MMPTrack, JRDB, DIVOTrack, EPFL Multi-camera Pedestrians, CAMPUS, MOT17/MOT20, DanceTrack, WILDTRACK, ClonedPerson, CCUP, MTA, WePerson, GPR+/FineGPR, Diff-Person, DeepFashion and DeepFashion2, Renderpeople scans, BEDLAM, and Unity Asset Store content. **This extends transitively to weights**: any checkpoint whose provenance traces to these corpora is out, and a permissive repo badge does not launder the data underneath it. Build the allowlist from what is provably clean (ImageNet, LVD-142M/DINOv2, MEVA, TVRID) rather than by clearing candidate checkpoints one at a time, and keep separate from it what is admitted for prototype-class training only under a recorded conflict note: **MEVID**, whose annotation release states no licence of its own and whose registry entry therefore carries `commercial_use: conflicting` with the note that silence is not a grant (`configs/datasets/mevid.yaml`), so every weight that data touches stays in the prototype weight class. **RandPerson** carries its own recorded caveat, whether the repository's Apache-2.0 licence reaches the off-repo image data, which is unconfirmed; **NVIDIA SmartSpaces** carries the responsible-AI statement and the 2026-09-03 mesh audit that excluded it from identity-discriminative embedder training.
5. **Corollary of the acquisition channel: no research-use agreement is signed on behalf of a commercial product** (POLICY.md, section 3). MMPTrack, DIVOTrack (which requires a faculty signature a company cannot honestly provide), and MTA all require attesting to research-only use. Those are not signed.
